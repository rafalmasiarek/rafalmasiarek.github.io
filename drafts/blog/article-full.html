Zero-downtime rollouts with Ansible and canary hosts

2026-10-18 ansible devops

Most config-management rollouts fail the same way: someone runs the playbook against the whole inventory at once, a bad assumption surfaces on host 40 of 60, and now a third of the fleet is in a half-applied state while the other two thirds are fine. The fix isn't a smarter playbook - it's never letting the blast radius be "everything" in the first place.

The canary group

Every inventory gets a canary group - one or two hosts per role, picked to be representative rather than special-cased. The playbook always targets canary first, with a hard stop on failure before anything else is touched. 

- hosts: canary
  serial: 1
  tasks:
    - include_tasks: deploy.yml

- hosts: all:!canary
  serial: "25%"
  tasks:
    - include_tasks: deploy.yml

The health-check gate

Between the canary play and the full rollout there's a gate task that polls the service's own health endpoint, not just "did the playbook exit 0". A host can apply a config change successfully and still be serving errors a minute later - the gate is what actually catches that.

If the canary host doesn't report healthy inside the timeout, the play fails before a single other host is touched. No judgment call, no "it's probably fine" - the gate decides.

The serial: "25%" on the remaining hosts keeps the same discipline going for the rest of the fleet: each batch has to clear the health gate before the next one starts.
